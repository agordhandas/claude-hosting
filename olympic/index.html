Olympic. Three landscapes in a single peninsula — alpine, rainforest, and coast — at the pace of a one-and-a-half year old.

A counter-clockwise loop through the Olympic Peninsula for a party of eight, designed around the realities of toddlers: short walks, frequent snacks, mild weather, and three of the most distinctive ecosystems in North America inside a single park boundary.

One park. Three worlds. The Olympic Peninsula is the only place in the lower 48 where you can stand on a glaciated alpine ridge in the morning, walk through temperate rainforest at lunch, and watch waves break on a Pacific sea stack at sunset.

i.

Alpine Ridge

5,200 ft up Hurricane Ridge, the Olympic Mountains spread in a 270° panorama. Wildflowers in July, marmots in the talus, deer that walk through the parking lot like they own it.

Picea engelmannii — Engelmann spruce
ii.

Rainforest

The Hoh receives twelve to fourteen feet of rain a year. Sitka spruce and western hemlock grow three hundred feet tall and are draped in clubmoss so thick the trees look upholstered.

Selaginella oregana — Oregon spikemoss
iii.

Wild Coast

Seventy-three miles of undeveloped Pacific shoreline. Driftwood the size of train cars. Sea stacks that look prehistoric. Fog that rolls in by 4 PM most summer afternoons. Tidepools at low tide.

Pisaster ochraceus — ochre sea star
Day One · Arrival

Up the
ridge.

Fly into Seattle, ferry across the Sound (kids will love it), drive to Port Angeles. Get up high your first afternoon while the weather's good — the mountains are often clouded in by morning two.

12:0090 min

Port Angeles & Visitor Center

About 2.5 hours from SEA via the Bainbridge ferry. Lunch in Port Angeles. Stop by the Olympic National Park Visitor Center on the way up — small kids' area, Junior Ranger booklets here, ranger talk on what's snowed in.

14:304 hrs

Hurricane Ridge

17 miles of switchbacks up to 5,200 ft and one of the great mountain panoramas in the country. Cold even in August — bring fleeces. Easy paved Cirque Rim Trail loop right at the visitor center. Deer in the parking lot, marmots if you're lucky.

Day Two · Lake & Hot Springs

Lake.
Falls. Soak.

Possibly the best single day of any national park trip with kids in this age range. Glacier-blue water, a real waterfall, and three hot mineral pools to end on. Nobody melts down today.

09:3090 min

Lake Crescent Lodge

30 minutes west of Port Angeles. Stunning glacier-fed lake; the water is genuinely Caribbean-blue from low nitrogen. Park at the lodge, skip rocks, walk the shore. Coffee and snacks at the lodge.

11:002 hrs

Marymere Falls

Trail starts at Lake Crescent. 1.8 miles round-trip through old-growth forest to a 90-foot waterfall. Mostly flat with one set of switchback stairs at the end. Doable for the 4 and 5 yr olds; toddlers in carriers. The forest itself is the magic — mossy, fairytale.

14:004 hrs

Sol Duc Hot Springs

Day-use access to the mineral hot springs — three pools at different temperatures plus a regular swimming pool. The kids will be in heaven. Cap the day with the easy 1.6 mi Sol Duc Falls trail nearby — one of the prettiest waterfalls in the park, very stroller-tolerant.

18:00—

Stay Lake Crescent

Drive 30 minutes east to Lake Crescent Lodge for the night, or back to Port Angeles. Tonight's the night to overnight in the park if you've booked it — sunset over the lake from the lodge porch is something else.

Day Three · Forest & Sea

Hoh in
the morning.

A long day with two of Olympic's signature experiences back to back: the temperate rainforest and the wild Pacific. Plan for ~5 hours of total driving. Pack snacks. Pack more snacks.

09:004 hrs

Hoh Rainforest

~2 hours from Lake Crescent. Critical: line at the entrance can hit 2 hours by 11 AM in summer. Arrive before 10 or you're stuck. Do the Hall of Mosses (0.8 mi, flat, the rainforest postcard) and the Spruce Nature Trail (1.2 mi). Toddlers walk parts; carriers for the rest.

14:0090 min

Forks (Lunch)

30 minutes from Hoh. Pacific Pizza is the kid-friendly play. If you have Twilight fans in the group, the gift shops play it up hard. Otherwise just refuel and push to the coast.

16:003 hrs

Rialto Beach

30 minutes from Forks. Otherworldly. Massive driftwood logs the kids will fort-build with for hours, sea stacks, bald eagles overhead. The beach is rocky pebbles, not sand. Stay through golden hour. Don't attempt the 4-mi Hole-in-the-Wall hike with this group; just beach time.

19:30—

Forks or Kalaloch (overnight)

Stay close — Kalaloch Lodge is on the coast and tomorrow's first stop is right there. Kalaloch books out 10+ months ahead; Forks has more cabin and motel options.

Day Four · Coast & Home

Tidepools
then Quinault.

A final coast morning, then start working the loop back toward Seattle via the south side of the peninsula. Tighter day — build in airport buffer and don't book an early flight.

10:002.5 hrs

Ruby Beach

20 minutes south of Forks. The iconic Olympic coast image — sea stacks, tidepools at low tide (check the chart), driftwood. Short steep-ish trail down to the beach (carriers needed). Pack a picnic. Better tidepools than Rialto if timed right.

13:303 hrs

Lake Quinault Lodge

1 hour south of Ruby Beach. Historic lodge on a beautiful lake. Lunch on the lawn. Easy nearby walks: the Rain Forest Nature Trail (0.5 mi loop, massive trees) or the Quinault Big Cedar — world's largest, short walk from the road. Then ~2.5 hr drive back to SEA.

Where to stay

Two bases. Three options.

NIGHTS 1–2★ TOP PICK

Lake Crescent Lodge

historic, rustic, on the water

Built in 1916, no TVs, shared bathrooms in the main lodge but private cabins available. The view from the porch is the reason to come. Restaurant is genuinely excellent. Marymere Falls trailhead is in the parking lot.

Books out 12 months in advance. Cabins go first.

~$280–$420 / night peak
NIGHTS 1–2 ALTEASIER

Port Angeles Inn

flexible, modern, good base

If Lake Crescent is full or you want predictable rooms with WiFi and TVs, base in Port Angeles. 30 minutes from Lake Crescent, full restaurant scene, easy grocery runs. Less atmosphere but more functional with kids.

Many options including Olympic Lodge, Red Lion, and Vrbos.

~$200–$350 / room / night
NIGHTS 3–4COAST

Kalaloch Lodge

on the bluff, ocean views

Bluff cabins overlooking the Pacific — the kids will lose their minds at sunset. Older property, expect quirks. Best position for hitting Ruby Beach in the morning. Restaurant on site so you don't need to drive for dinner.

Books 13 months out. If full: Forks has cabin/motel options 30 min away.

~$320–$500 / night
Field Notes

Things that matter more than people say.

i.

Hoh Rainforest Entry Line

The single biggest scheduling risk of the trip. Park rangers stop traffic 5 miles before the visitor center; from that point, posted waits hit 2 hours by mid-morning in summer. Arrive before 10 AM or visit late afternoon (after 3 PM). The middle of the day is the trap.

ii.

Lodging Books a Year Out

In-park lodges (Lake Crescent, Sol Duc, Kalaloch, Lake Quinault) all book 12–13 months ahead for July/August. Set a calendar reminder. If you missed the window, check daily for cancellations — they happen.

iii.

Tide Charts

Tidepools at Ruby and Rialto are only good at low tide. Download the NOAA tide app or check Tides.net. Aim for low tide windows that line up with daylight on day 3 or 4 — that's the difference between "saw some rocks" and "kids found a sea star."

iv.

The Bainbridge Ferry

The Seattle → Bainbridge Island ferry saves driving time and is genuinely fun for kids — they can be out of the car on the upper deck. ~35 min crossing, runs every 50 min. Walk-on passengers always get on; cars sometimes wait a sailing in summer.

v.

What to Pack

Rain layers (rainforest is rainy even in August), warm fleece for Hurricane Ridge (50°F at the top), water shoes for Ruby tidepools, swimsuits for Sol Duc and Lake Crescent, two kid-carrier backpacks. Strollers work at the rainforest visitor center and not much else.

vi.

Bears Are Real but Calm

Olympic has black bears, not grizzlies. Keep food locked in the car at trailheads, don't leave snacks in strollers at picnic areas, and you'll be fine. Bear spray is overkill here unless you're backpacking.
